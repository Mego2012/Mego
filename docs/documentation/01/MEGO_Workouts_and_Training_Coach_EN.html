1. Data protection

  • No schema change (database version 2). Training data is stored as new records in the existing synced key/value table:

    Record key Contents
    program:<id> a training program
    activeProgram which program is active
    workout:<id> one per session
    exercise:<id> a custom exercise
    recovery:<date> a daily recovery log
    preferredExercises exercises you marked as preferred

    The live server rules already restrict these records to their owner. Tests confirm that account B gets 403 and sees an empty history.

  • Pre-update backup. The first start of release 1.3-training saves a full snapshot first, once per release. Rotation never removes it.

  • The backup is restorable. A test restores a pre-update backup into an empty database and gets identical diary entries, foods and body records.

  • Deleting a workout removes only that session. Deleting a program never touches workout history, and the active program cannot be deleted. Editing a program never changes logged sessions.

2. Exercise library

  • 54 built-in exercises.
  • Each has an Arabic and English name, primary and secondary muscles, equipment, a category (compound or isolation), a recommended sets × reps and rest, instructions in both languages, and alternatives.
  • Alternatives are always filtered to the equipment you have.
  • You can add custom exercises.
  • No exercise photos: no freely licensed set of these movements was available. A drawn diagram highlights the target muscles instead.

3. Program generator (keeps your split)

The input is your split (default: the owner's 6 days plus rest, mapped to the training weekdays already in Settings: Saturday … Thursday, with Friday as rest), plus:

  • experience
  • available equipment
  • weekly sets per muscle (beginner 10 / intermediate 14 / advanced 16)
  • pull-ups and dips per week
  • preferred exercises
  • exercises logged in the last 6 weeks

Rules:

  • The split is never changed. "Keep my current split" is the default.
  • Volume. Per muscle: sets per session = weekly target ÷ times trained per week, capped at 10 per session for a big muscle (8 when the day trains 3 or more muscles) and 6 for a small one. Each day is capped at 24 sets, trimming isolation sets first. The aim is roughly 10–20 hard sets per muscle per week.
  • Exercise choice. One compound first, chosen for strength retention in a deficit. Then a second compound angle or an isolation exercise. Near-duplicates of the same type are never put on the same day (for example two squat variants). A muscle's second day of the week prefers variations not yet used. Exercises you already train or marked as preferred are kept.
  • Pull-ups and dips go first on back and triceps days. Any extra weekly frequency goes on biceps days (pull-ups) and chest days (dips), at most once per day. With the owner's split, up to 3 dips per week fit.
  • Rep ranges and effort. Each exercise's recommended range (heavy compounds 5–10, isolation 10–20). Target RIR is 2 for heavy compounds and 1 for isolation. Rest is 60–180 s.
  • Every exercise shows why it was chosen. The result is saved only as a new program, and only if you choose to.

4. Session logging

Each set records weight, reps, RIR (0–5+), RPE (optional) and done. Each exercise can be marked skipped and have notes. Ticking a set starts its rest timer. Each exercise shows its last performance next to the current one. Weights are prefilled from the progression advice, and you can change everything.

5. Strength analytics

  • e1RM (Epley): w × (1 + reps/30), using sets of 12 reps or fewer. For body-weight exercises the load is body weight plus added weight.
  • Volume: Σ load × reps.
  • Personal records: best e1RM, heaviest weight, most reps in a set, best session volume.
  • Trend: the mean e1RM of the last 3 sessions compared with the 3 before it.
    • more than +2 % → improving
    • less than −2 % → declining
    • otherwise stable
    • fewer than 4 sessions → insufficient data
  • Weekly sets per muscle: primary 1 set, secondary ½. The warning line is above max(20, goal + 6).

6. Progression (double progression)

All prescribed sets reach the top of the rep range:

  • RIR ≥ 1 on every set (or RPE ≤ 9): increase weight, high confidence. Step: +2.5 kg; +5 kg for lower-body compounds at 100 kg or more; +1 kg under 20 kg.
  • RIR/RPE not recorded: increase weight, medium confidence, and the app says why.
  • Reached at failure (RIR 0 / RPE 10): keep the weight.

Otherwise:

  • All sets within the range: add reps at the same weight. Completing a workout alone never raises the weight.
  • Below the range once: keep the weight.
  • Below the range twice in a row: reduce by about 5 %.
  • Declining trend with poor recovery, or a drop of more than 5 %: reduce by about 10 %.
  • No new best in the last 3 of 6 or more sessions: consider replacing the exercise; alternatives are listed.

Nothing changes the program automatically.

7. Recovery

The daily log is optional: sleep hours, plus soreness, energy and fatigue on a 1–5 scale. Warnings cover:

  • average sleep under 6.5 h
  • soreness of 4 or more
  • energy of 2 or less
  • fatigue of 4 or more
  • 7 or more training days in a row
  • very high weekly volume
  • 2 or more exercises declining
  • cardio above twice the plan while leg performance declines

These are not a medical assessment, and nothing is diagnosed.

8. Calories (no double counting, no surplus on fat loss)

  • Saved targets (for example the owner's 2,110 kcal) are never changed. A test confirms this.
  • Your planned resistance days per week are inside the TDEE through the activity level, so planned workouts add nothing.
  • Only sessions beyond the plan in the last 7 days count: net energy (3.5 − 1 MET) × kg × duration. They are added back with the same goal-based share and the same 400 kcal/day cap as cardio, and each step is explained on Home.
  • For fat-loss goals:
    • Any suggested calorie increase (Goals recommendations) is limited so the target stays at or below 90 % of TDEE, so the app never suggests a surplus. If there is no room, it recommends recovery and protein instead.
    • The daily adjusted target is limited to 90 % of that day's expenditure (TDEE + extra exercise), so exercise never turns a day into maintenance or surplus.

9. Coach (rule-based, on the phone, free)

The coach answers these questions from logged data only, each with a confidence level and the data it is based on:

  • What should I train today?
  • What weight should I use today?
  • Am I progressing?
  • Should I increase my weight?
  • Why has my performance declined? It lists only factors present in the data.
  • Am I doing too many sets?
  • Is my weekly volume sufficient?
  • Which muscles need more attention?
  • Should I take an extra rest day?

When data is missing, it says so. It uses no external AI service and has no cost.

Nutrition × training checks:

  • the deficit vs TDEE (10–25 % is appropriate)
  • the loss rate (0.25–1 % per week)
  • protein (1.8 g/kg or more)
  • carbohydrates (under 2 g/kg → place carbohydrates around training, same calories)

10. Weekly review

The review covers last week, or this week so far. It has 8 observations:

  1. nutrition adherence
  2. weight trend
  3. waist
  4. strength (PRs, trends)
  5. workout consistency
  6. cardio
  7. recovery
  8. goal progress

Recommendations are listed separately, as suggestions only.

11. Reports and dashboards

  • Weekly and monthly reports add a Workouts card: sessions vs planned, consistency, hard sets, volume, new PRs, strength trends and sets per muscle.
  • Home shows today's workout.
  • Workouts → Overview is the unified dashboard: body, today's nutrition, week, strength, muscle volume, recovery and what to focus on.
