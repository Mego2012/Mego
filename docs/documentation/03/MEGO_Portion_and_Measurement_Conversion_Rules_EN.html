Purpose and scope

This document defines exactly how MEGO turns a quantity typed by the user (grams, kilograms, millilitres, litres, cups, tablespoons, teaspoons, pieces, slices or servings) into grams, and how grams become calories and macronutrients. It also covers how nutrition-label values entered "per 100 ml" or "per serving" are converted to the internal per-100 g basis, and the rounding rules. All rules are taken from src/lib/nutrition.ts, src/pages/Foods.tsx (food editor), src/components/FoodPicker.tsx, src/lib/refFoods.ts and src/lib/barcode.ts.

1. Core principle: everything is stored per 100 g

Every food record has a per100 nutrient set (kcal, protein, carbs, fat and optionally fiber, sugar, sodium in mg, satFat) expressed per 100 g of the food in the state stored on the record (raw, cooked or other). When a food is logged, MEGO:

  1. converts the quantity + unit to grams with toGrams();
  2. scales the per-100 g values by grams / 100 (scaleMacros);
  3. stores the result with 0.01 precision (preciseMacros) together with a snapshot of the basis (EntryBasis: per100, pieceGrams, gramsPerMl, servings).

Because the snapshot is stored on the diary entry, later edits to the food never change past entries. The code comment on toGrams states the second fundamental rule: MEGO never converts between raw and cooked weights. Raw rice and cooked rice are separate records with separate values; the user must pick the record that matches what they weighed.

 quantity + unit ──► toGrams(basis) ──► grams ──► per100 × grams/100 ──► macros (0.01 precision)
                       │                                             └─► snapshot stored on the entry
                       └─ uses ONLY values stored on the food: gramsPerMl, pieceGrams, servings

The density used is basis.gramsPerMl when it is a positive number; otherwise 1. A quantity that is not a finite number greater than zero is rejected (UnitError('invalidQuantity')).

3. Which units are offered for a food

availableUnits(basis) decides which units the quantity form shows:

  • A recipe basis with only perServing (no per100): only serving.
  • Every other food: g, kg, ml, l, cup, tbsp, tsp.
  • Plus piece and slice only when pieceGrams > 0.
  • Plus serving only when the food has at least one serving defined.

gramsPerUnit() calculates the grams in one unit (rounded to 0.1 g) for labels such as "1 cup = 240 g"; it returns nothing when the unit cannot be converted.

4. Density (gramsPerMl) and the 1 ml = 1 g assumption

Volume units (ml, l, cup, tbsp, tsp) need a density to become grams. The food field gramsPerMl ("Density (g per ml)" in the food editor) holds it. When it is missing, MEGO assumes 1 ml = 1 g and tells the user: the quantity form shows "No density set — 1 ml is counted as 1 g." whenever the unit is ml, tbsp or tsp and the food has no density; the food editor shows "No density entered: 1 ml = 1 g is assumed." when values are typed per 100 ml without a density.

Consequences: for water-like foods (milk, juices) the assumption is close; for oils (density below 1) and syrups or honey (density above 1) it is less accurate, so the user should enter the density. No density values are built into the app — the field is filled only by the user (or by an import/backup that carries it). USDA reference foods do not set gramsPerMl; instead their published household portions become servings (section 5).

Note: the quantity form shows the density hint for ml, tbsp and tsp; it does not show it for cup and l, although the same assumption applies to them in toGrams().

5. Piece / slice weight and servings

5.1 Piece and slice

pieceGrams is the weight of one piece, slice or standard portion (Hub column "وزن الحبة/الحصة", structured column piece_g). Both "piece" and "slice" use the same value. Without it, piece/slice logging throws UnitError('needsPieceWeight'). Sources of pieceGrams:

  • the owner's Hub / CSV / Excel import (piece column; only values > 0 are kept);
  • the food editor field "Piece / slice weight (g)";
  • Open Food Facts: the product's serving_quantity is proposed as the piece weight in the product form (the user can change it).

5.2 Servings

A food can have a list of named servings (CustomServing: id, name, grams). They come from:

  • USDA reference foods: when a USDA food is first used, up to six published household portions (label ≤ 60 characters, gram weight > 0) are copied as servings by materializeRefFood();
  • the food editor (custom servings list);
  • a nutrition label entered "per serving": the label serving is added automatically as a serving named "Label serving" (if no serving with the same grams exists).

6. Recipes (per-serving basis)

A recipe entry uses a basis with perServing and no per100. macrosFor() then multiplies the per-serving values by the number of servings, and the entry's grams are 0 in this path. If a basis has neither per100 nor perServing, the error needsRecipe ("Missing nutrition basis") is raised.

7. Label conversion in the food editor

In the food editor ("New food" / "Edit food") the field "Values entered per" has three options. Whatever is chosen, the values are saved per 100 g. The conversion factor is computed in FoodEditor:

OptionFactor applied to every typed valueNotes
per 100 g (default)× 1—
per 100 ml× 1 / densitydensity = the Density field, or 1 when empty (hint shown)
per serving (nutrition label)× 100 / SS = "Serving size" in grams; Save is disabled while S is not > 0

Each converted value is rounded to 2 decimals. The factor applies to kcal, protein, carbs, fat and, when filled, fiber, sugar, satFat and sodium. The editor also shows the 4/4/9 calculated energy and an energy explanation (see MEGO_Food_Data_Validation_and_Quality_Control).

Open Food Facts products are always read from the _100g nutriment fields; energy in kJ is converted with ÷ 4.184 when kcal is absent, and sodium is converted from g to mg (× 1000).

8. Rounding rules

Displayed valueswhole kcal; 0.1 g for protein, carbs, fat and optional nutrientsroundMacros

"1 unit = x g" labels0.1 ggramsPerUnit
Label conversion in the editor0.01round(x × factor, 2)
Coach portion suggestionsmultiples of 5 g, minimum 5 groundG in coach.ts

round(n, digits) uses Math.round((n + Number.EPSILON) × 10^digits) / 10^digits. Day totals are summed from the precise values and rounded only for display (a unit test shows ten entries of ≈2.7 g tomato giving 5 kcal, exact 4.86).
